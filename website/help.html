Basics

Getting started

Sovereign Tax runs entirely on your computer. There is no account and no cloud: your data is stored locally and encrypted with a key derived from your PIN. On first launch you accept the Terms of Service and choose a 4–6 digit PIN; after that, the app opens to a lock screen.

Keep backups

Because nothing is stored in the cloud, your backups are the only copy of your data outside this computer. Create one in Settings → Backup & Restore after each work session (see Settings, backups and privacy).

The workflow at a glance

1

Bring in your history

Import a CSV from each exchange or wallet (Import), and add anything missing by hand (Add Transaction). You need every purchase that funds a later sale, including prior years.

2

Record transfers between your wallets

For every move between wallets you own, make sure there is a Transfer In on the receiving wallet and assign its source wallet. This is what moves your cost basis. See Transfers between your wallets.

3

Choose lots for your sales

Sales use FIFO by default. To pick lots yourself, or let the app pick the lowest-tax lots, use Lots or Optimize in Transactions. See FIFO, Specific ID and Optimize.

4

Review, then export

Work through the Review checklist until it's all green, then export Form 8949 (PDF or CSV) or a TurboTax file from Tax Report. Create a backup.

Key idea

Wallets and exchanges

From January 1, 2025, IRS rules require cost basis to be tracked per wallet or account (Treasury Reg. §1.1012-1(j)). When you sell bitcoin, the cost basis has to come from lots held in the wallet you sold from. So every transaction in Sovereign Tax belongs to a wallet, and it has two name fields:

FieldWhat it meansUsed for
Wallet The wallet or account this record's coins are in. On a Transfer Out, that's the wallet the coins left. On a Transfer In, it's the wallet they arrived in. Cost basis. Holdings, per-wallet lot selection and wallet balances are all keyed by Wallet.
Exchange A label for the platform or account: "Swan", "ColdCard", an LLC's name. Display and grouping. Cost basis ignores it unless Wallet is blank, in which case it's used as the wallet name.
  • Use one exact name per wallet, everywhere. Names are case-insensitive ("Coinbase" and "coinbase" are the same wallet), but "Coinbase" and "Coinbase Pro" are two different wallets.
  • Separate accounts need separate names. If you have two accounts at the same exchange, or accounts for different entities, give each one its own wallet name, for example "Swan – Personal" and "Swan – LLC A".
  • A hardware wallet is a wallet too, e.g. "ColdCard Personal".
Data

Importing CSV files

Export a CSV from your exchange or wallet software, then open Import. Supported out of the box: Coinbase, Kraken, Gemini, Strike, Cash App, River, Swan, Bisq, and any CSV with date, amount and price (or total) columns.

1

Enter the Wallet / Exchange Name

This field is required, and every row in the file is tagged with this name as its wallet. Wallet or account columns inside the CSV are not used. So import each account's CSV separately, under that account's own name, and reuse exactly the same name for later imports. The name also remembers your column mapping for next time.

2

Choose the file and check the mapping

Columns are detected automatically. Check the Column Mapping and Data Preview, and fix anything that wasn't recognized. Required: Date, Amount (BTC), and Price or Total. Type, Fee and Notes are optional.

  • No Type column? Pick a default type (Buy, Sell, Transfer In, Transfer Out, Donation) for every row.
  • Total already includes fees? Tick The Total column already includes fees so the fee isn't counted twice. It's detected automatically for headers like Coinbase's "Total (inclusive of fees and/or spread)".
  • Files with separate Sent and Received columns are handled automatically.
3

Import

Only bitcoin rows are imported; other assets are skipped. The same file can't be imported twice, and individual duplicate transactions are skipped too.

Transfers in your CSVs

An exchange export usually shows withdrawals ("Send", "Withdrawal") and imports them as Transfer Out. A wallet export shows deposits ("Receive") and imports them as Transfer In. After importing, assign a source wallet to each Transfer In; see Transfers.

Data

Adding and editing transactions

Add Transaction records anything that isn't in a CSV: a Buy, Sell, Transfer In, Transfer Out or Donation.

  • Total USD is the amount before fees. Leave it blank to use Amount × Price. Enter the fee separately: it's added to your cost basis on a buy and subtracted from your proceeds on a sale. If the Total looks like it already includes the fee, the app warns you and offers to correct it.
  • Income (mining, rewards, interest, forks) is a Buy with an Income Type. Its fair market value becomes both income and cost basis.
  • Donations use the fair market value per BTC on the donation date.
  • Transfers: the form explains what Wallet means for each side. Set Wallet to the wallet the coins left (Transfer Out) or arrived in (Transfer In).
  • Sells with Specific ID: choose Specific ID as the method, pick the wallet, then choose the lots. See Specific ID.

In Transactions you can search, filter by type and year, sort by any column, use Edit or delete on any row, and select several rows to delete them or change their Exchange together. Export CSV saves your full transaction list.

Bulk "Change Exchange" also renames the wallet

On rows where Wallet and Exchange were identical, which is the case for every imported row, bulk Change Exchange updates the Wallet too, so those lots move to the new name. That's usually what you want. Check Holdings afterwards.

Transfers

Transfers between your wallets

Moving bitcoin between wallets you own (exchange to hardware wallet, hardware wallet to another exchange) isn't taxable. The cost basis and holding period travel with the coins. Because cost basis is tracked per wallet, the app needs to know which lots moved where, so that when you later sell from the new wallet, the right lots are there.

The rule in one line

A Transfer In on the receiving wallet, with its source wallet assigned, is what moves cost basis. A Transfer Out on the sending wallet is optional: it completes your paper trail but doesn't move any lots by itself.

Recording a transfer, step by step

1

Add (or import) the Transfer In

Wallet = the wallet the coins arrived in (e.g. "ColdCard Personal"). Amount = the amount received. Exchange is just a label; use whatever describes it ("ColdCard", or the account's name).

2

Assign its source wallet

In Transactions, unassigned Transfer Ins show a red Assign button. Click it and choose the wallet the coins came from. The list shows each wallet's balance at the time of the transfer, and a wallet may be marked Suggested when a matching withdrawal was found (see Reconciliation).

Which lots moved? By default the oldest lots move first (FIFO). To choose, tick Choose specific lots to transfer and pick lots. The picker's Optimize button can pick for you. Any amount you don't pick is filled FIFO.

After saving, the row shows ← source and whether the lots were chosen by Specific ID or FIFO. Click it to change the assignment.

3

Optional: the Transfer Out

Wallet = the wallet the coins left (e.g. "Swan Personal"), with the same date and the amount sent. Exchange-exported withdrawals come in as Transfer Outs automatically. The Reconciliation page pairs it with its Transfer In.

Partial transfers

A Transfer In moves only its own amount, and everything else stays in the source wallet. Moving 0.3 of your 1.0 BTC to another wallet is simply a 0.3 BTC Transfer In with its source assigned. Use Choose specific lots to transfer when it matters which lots moved.

Common mistake

Setting a Transfer Out's Wallet to the destination. The Transfer Out always belongs to the wallet the coins left. In the March row above, that's ColdCard Personal, not SafeExchange Personal. The Reconciliation page warns you when both sides of a pair are in the same wallet.

Several accounts or entities (for example, LLCs)

  • Give every wallet a distinct name per account and per entity: "Swan – LLC A", "ColdCard – LLC A", "Swan – LLC B"…
  • Always assign the source wallet on each Transfer In. That's what keeps one entity's cost basis from moving into another entity's wallet.
  • When two accounts send the identical amount on the same day, Reconciliation pairs each with its own deposit if the records say which is which: either an assigned source wallet, or the account's label in Exchange on both sides. If nothing distinguishes them, both are marked Ambiguous and left for you to match. The app never guesses.

Fixing mistakes — what affects cost basis

ChangeAffects cost basis?
Any field on a Transfer OutNo. Transfer Outs don't move lots.
An Exchange label, with the Wallet unchangedNo.
A Transfer In's Wallet or source walletYes. The lots are re-routed. Later sales whose Specific ID picks used lots that moved show a warning and fall back to FIFO, so re-run Optimize for that year.
A Buy's or Sell's WalletYes. It changes which wallet the lots are in or come from.
Bulk Change ExchangePossibly. It also renames the Wallet where Wallet and Exchange were identical, so check Holdings.
Transfers

The Reconciliation page

Reconciliation checks your paper trail: it pairs each Transfer Out with its Transfer In and shows a balance for every wallet. Your tax numbers don't depend on this page, because cost basis moves only through the source wallets you assign. Pairs found here do power the Suggested source wallet in Assign.

How pairs are matched

A Transfer Out and a Transfer In are paired when the amounts agree (the received amount may be slightly lower, for the miner fee) and the deposit arrives 0–7 days later in a different wallet. When several deposits could fit one withdrawal, the app prefers, in order:

  1. the deposit whose assigned source wallet is this withdrawal's wallet;
  2. the deposit labeled with the same account (the same Exchange label, or an Exchange label naming the source wallet);
  3. the smallest implied fee, then the fewest days.

If two pairings between different wallets are still equally good, nothing is paired and both are marked Ambiguous.

You're in control

  • Manual matching: select one outgoing and one incoming transfer in the Unmatched lists, then Confirm Match. Manual matches always take priority over automatic ones.
  • Unmatch works on every pair. Both transfers move to the Unmatched lists, and auto-match leaves them alone from then on. Let auto-match pair them again undoes this.
  • Auto-match transfers (the switch at the top) can be turned off to pair everything by hand.

Wallet balances

Computed per wallet, using the same wallets as Holdings, from your records: buys and deposits add; sells, donations and withdrawals subtract. A Transfer In with a source wallet but no Transfer Out counts as leaving the source wallet. When every transfer is recorded consistently, these match your Holdings. A negative balance usually means a missing buy or transfer.

Tax

FIFO, Specific ID and Optimize

Sovereign Tax supports the two IRS-permitted methods. FIFO (first in, first out) is the default: each sale uses the oldest lots in its wallet. Specific Identification lets you choose exactly which lots each sale or donation uses, but it must be elected before the disposal.

  • One sale: in Transactions, click Lots on a sell or donation. It shows Lots ✓ once lots are chosen. Or choose Specific ID when you add the sale.
  • A whole year: Optimize (N) assigns the lowest-tax lots (losses first, then the smallest gains) to every sale and donation in the selected year that doesn't have a Specific ID election yet. It shows the FIFO and optimized totals before you apply anything.
  • Undo: Revert to FIFO removes all of the selected year's Specific ID elections.
  • Per-wallet rule: lots come from the wallet you sell from. If a sale's wallet has no lots, usually because a Transfer In is missing its source wallet, it's marked with ⚠️ as a wallet mismatch. Fix the transfer and re-optimize.
  • Simulate Sale previews a hypothetical sale without recording anything. With Specific ID, the lots you pick carry over to Add Transaction. Compare Methods shows FIFO against optimal Specific ID.
Tax

Review checklist

Review is a guided checklist for the selected tax year. Fix these before exporting:

  1. Assign source wallets: every Transfer In needs one. You can assign right from the checklist.
  2. Resolve wallet mismatches: sales that had to use lots from another wallet. This is usually a missing source wallet on a transfer.
  3. Optimize lot selections (optional): apply Specific ID to lower your tax. You can always revert to FIFO.

When everything is green, your data is ready for the Tax Report.

Tax

Tax Report and exports

Tax Report shows Form 8949 and Schedule D figures for the selected year, split into short-term (held one year or less) and long-term. Sales that span both are split automatically. Specific ID elections from Transactions are applied automatically. Prior-year capital loss carryforwards (short-term and long-term) are entered in Settings → Tax Settings.

Views

Income, donations and other views

  • Income: mining, rewards, interest and fork income, taxed as ordinary income at fair market value on the date received (Schedule 1).
  • Holdings: your current lots with cost basis and unrealized gain or loss. Filter by wallet.
  • Lot Maturity: when each lot becomes long-term.
  • Tax Harvesting: lots trading below their cost basis that could offset this year's gains.
  • Multi-Year: gains and losses across all your tax years.
  • Audit Log: a timestamped record of every import, edit, deletion and lot election.
Settings

Settings, backups and privacy

  • Backup & Restore: create a password-protected backup file. You'll need that password to restore it. Backups you create are also listed inside the app, and you can restore from a file or from that list.
  • Live BTC Price: on, the app fetches prices from CoinGecko. Off, it runs fully offline.
  • Hide Amounts blurs all BTC and USD values, e.g. for screen sharing. PIN Lock changes your PIN.
  • Theme: System, Light or Dark.
  • Permanently Delete All Data erases everything (type DELETE to confirm). Create a backup first.
Recovery

Starting over for a tax year

Usually you don't need to: correcting a Transfer Out or an Exchange label doesn't touch your cost basis (see Fixing mistakes). If you do want a clean slate for a year's lot choices:

1

Back up

Settings → Backup & Restore.

2

Clear the year

In Transactions, select the year. Revert to FIFO removes that year's sale and donation elections. Clear Assignments removes that year's Transfer In source wallets and lot picks.

3

Re-assign transfers

Click Assign on each Transfer In, choose its source wallet, and pick lots if you want.

4

Re-optimize

Click Optimize for the year's sales, then work through Review.

Work from the oldest year forward, because each year's lot choices affect which lots remain for the next.
